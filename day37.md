# Day 37 – Task Compass: Organizational Thinking

## Objective
Today's task focused on understanding how work flows across different teams in an organization using the Task Compass application generated with Claude AI. 
The objective was to identify ownership, route tasks correctly, collaborate across departments, and analyze organizational thinking.

# Stage 1 – Who Owns This?

In this stage, I identified the correct department responsible for handling different workplace scenarios.

### Examples
- Checkout page performance issue → Backend Developer
- Production outage → DevOps Engineer
- Customer refund request → Customer Support

### Key Learning
Ownership is about accountability. The team that notices a problem is not always the team responsible for fixing it.

---

# Stage 2 – Task Routing

This stage focused on routing tasks through the correct departments in the proper sequence.

### Examples
- Landing page development workflow
- Security vulnerability resolution
- Customer-reported bug workflow

### Key Learning
A structured workflow improves communication, reduces delays, and ensures every team contributes at the right stage.

---

# Stage 3 – Collaboration Challenge

This stage required selecting the appropriate teams for solving organization-wide challenges.

### Examples
- Negative customer reviews after an update
- Customer satisfaction drop
- Major feature delivery delay

### Key Learning
Complex business problems require cross-functional collaboration between Product, Engineering, QA, Support, UX, Marketing, and other teams.

# Final Reflection

## What I Understood Well
I consistently identified the correct primary owner and understood where accountability belongs within an organization.

## Area for Improvement
I learned to avoid assigning responsibility to everyone and instead focus on the team that truly owns the issue.

## Collaboration Insight
Most real-world problems require multiple departments working together rather than a single team.

## Biggest Takeaway
The person who reports a problem is often different from the person responsible for solving it. Clear ownership, proper task routing,
and effective collaboration are essential for successful organizations.

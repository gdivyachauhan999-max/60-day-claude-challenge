

# Day 3 - Role Based Prompting

## Question
How can I improve my chances of getting a software developer job in 6 months?

## Normal Response:

Here's a focused 6-month plan to maximize your chances:
Months 1–2: Build a Strong Foundation
Sharpen core skills

Pick one language and go deep (Python, JavaScript, or Java are most hireable)
Master data structures & algorithms — do 2–3 LeetCode problems daily (easy → medium)
Learn system design basics (read Designing Data-Intensive Applications)

Clean up your online presence

GitHub: make it active with green squares — commit something every day
LinkedIn: complete profile, add a headline like "Aspiring Software Engineer | Python | React"
Portfolio site: even a simple one signals professionalism
Months 3–4: Build Projects That Matter
2–3 strong portfolio projects beat 10 mediocre ones. Each project should:

Solve a real problem (not just a tutorial clone)
Have a live demo + clean README
Show a full stack: frontend, backend, and a database

Good project ideas: expense tracker, job application tracker, a simple SaaS tool in your domain of interest.

Months 5–6: Apply Aggressively & Interview Prep
Applications

Apply to 10–15 roles per week — volume matters
Prioritize startups (less competition, faster processes) alongside big tech
Tailor your resume for each job's keywords (ATS systems filter heavily)

Interview prep

Mock interviews: use Pramp, Interviewing.io, or ask friends
Behavioral questions: prepare STAR-format answers for 10 common scenarios
Do timed coding challenges under pressure


Habits That Compound the Whole Time
HabitWhy It HelpsNetwork weekly (LinkedIn, meetups, Discord)~70% of jobs are filled through connectionsContribute to open sourceShows real-world collaboration skillsWrite about what you learn (blog/LinkedIn posts)Builds visibility and cements knowledgeApply even if you meet only 60% of requirementsMost listings are wish lists, not hard requirements

The Most Important Things

Consistency beats intensity — 2 hours daily > 14-hour weekend marathons
Apply early and often — don't wait until you feel "ready"
Referrals are gold — one warm intro is worth 50 cold applications





## Founder Persona Response:

What I Actually Look For When Hiring
The brutal truth: I don't care about your degree or certifications. I care about three things — can you ship, can you figure things out, and will you make my team better?

What Gets You Hired at a Startup in 6 Months
1. Build Something Real. Ship It.
Don't build another to-do app. Build something with actual users — even 10 friends using it counts. When you tell me "I built X and 15 people use it daily," that's more impressive than any bootcamp certificate. I want to see you've felt the pain of real users breaking your code.
2. Learn in Public
Tweet/post your progress. Write short LinkedIn posts like "Day 30: Here's what I learned about APIs this week." I've hired people directly from seeing their posts. It shows initiative, communication skills, and consistency — all things I desperately need on a small team.
3. Target Startups at Seed/Series A Stage
Big tech has 500 applicants per role. A 10-person startup gets 20. Your odds are 25x better. Plus, you'll learn faster in 6 months at a startup than 2 years at a big company.
4. Cold Email Founders Directly
Skip the job portal. Find a startup you like on Product Hunt or AngelList. Email the founder directly:

"Hi [Name], I've been using [product] — I noticed [specific issue/feature idea]. I'm a developer looking to join an early-stage team. I built [X project] that does something similar. Would love to contribute."

That email gets read. A LinkedIn application does not.
5. Contribute to Their Codebase Before Applying
Find a startup's open-source repo or public project. Fix a bug. Submit a PR. Then email them. I once hired someone within 48 hours because they sent me an unsolicited bug fix. That's the mindset I want on my team.

The Stack That Gets You Hired Fast
Don't try to learn everything. Pick this and go deep:

Frontend: React
Backend: Node.js or Python (FastAPI)
Database: PostgreSQL
Deployment: Vercel + Railway or AWS basics
Bonus: Any AI/LLM integration experience — this is a superpower right now


Month-by-Month Founder Lens
MonthFocus1–2Get solid on the core stack. Build one ugly but working project3Rebuild it properly. Make it live. Get real users4Start talking to founders. Go to startup meetups. Be visible5Cold outreach to 5 startups/week. Offer to work for free for 2 weeks6Convert a trial into an offer. You'll have 2–3 options by now

The Offer-Free Trial Trick
Offer to work 2 weeks unpaid on a specific problem. Say: "Give me a real task. If I deliver, consider hiring me." Most founders will say yes — it's zero risk for them. And if you're good, you're hired. I've done this with 3 of my best hires.

What Kills Your Chances

Generic resumes with no projects
Saying "I know a little of everything" — pick a lane
Only applying through job portals
Waiting until you're "ready" — you never feel ready, ship anyway


The developers I've hired in 6 months or less all had one thing in common: they acted like they were already on the team before I hired them. Show that hunger, and the job follows.

## Developer Persona Response:

The Hard Truth First
Most candidates fail not because they can't code, but because they can't demonstrate they can code under pressure, in a team, on real problems. That's what 6 months should fix.

Technical Skills — Go Deep, Not Wide
DSA is Non-Negotiable
Month 1-2:  Arrays, Strings, HashMaps, Two Pointers, Sliding Window
Month 3-4:  Trees, Graphs, BFS/DFS, Dynamic Programming (basics)
Month 5-6:  System Design, Advanced DP, Mock Interviews

Do LeetCode — 150+ problems minimum (NeetCode 150 is the best structured list)
Time yourself. 20 min easy, 35 min medium. No peeking.
Understand patterns, don't memorize solutions

Pick One Stack and Go Production-Level Deep
Don't just know React — know why useEffect runs twice in dev, how the virtual DOM diffing works, when to use Context vs Redux. Interviewers at good companies go deep.
Most hireable combos right now:

React + Node.js + PostgreSQL
Python (FastAPI/Django) + React + PostgreSQL
Any of the above + basic AWS/Docker

Code Quality Matters More Than You Think
Write code like someone else has to maintain it:

Meaningful variable names
Small, single-responsibility functions
Write tests (even basic ones — most candidates write zero)
Use Git properly: feature branches, clean commit messages


System Design — The Differentiator
Most junior candidates completely ignore this. It's how you stand out:

Learn the basics: Load balancers, caching (Redis), databases (SQL vs NoSQL), REST vs GraphQL, message queues
Study real architectures: How does Twitter's feed work? How does Uber match drivers?
Resource: System Design Primer (free on GitHub) + Grokking System Design

Even for junior roles, showing you think about scale impresses interviewers.

Your Portfolio — Quality Over Quantity
3 strong projects beat 10 tutorial clones. Each project must have:
✅ Live deployed URL (not localhost screenshots)
✅ Clean GitHub repo with a proper README
✅ Solves a real problem
✅ Shows full stack: UI + API + Database
✅ Has at least basic error handling and input validation
Project ideas that actually impress:

A tool you personally needed and built
Something with an external API integration
Anything with real users (even 5 users is huge)
A small CLI tool or dev utility (shows you think like an engineer)


The Resume — It's a Filtering Document
Your resume has 6 seconds to pass the first scan. Senior engineers reviewing resumes look for:
❌ "Familiar with React"       → ✅ "Built X with React, used by Y users"
❌ "Worked on backend APIs"    → ✅ "Designed REST API handling 10k req/day"
❌ "Helped with database"      → ✅ "Optimized SQL queries, reducing load time by 40%"
Always quantify. Always show impact.

One page only
No fluff — no "hardworking team player"
Tailor it per job — match the job description's keywords exactly (ATS systems are brutal)


The Interview Process — Know What's Coming
Most companies run this gauntlet:
1. Resume Screen      → ATS + Recruiter (keyword matching)
2. Phone Screen       → 1 easy coding problem + background questions
3. Technical Round    → 2 medium LC problems in 45 mins
4. System Design      → Design a URL shortener / chat app / feed
5. Behavioral Round   → STAR format stories
6. Offer
Prepare for ALL stages, not just coding.
Behavioral questions trip up great engineers constantly. Prepare 8–10 STAR stories covering: leadership, conflict, failure, tight deadline, debugging a hard bug, disagreement with a teammate.

Networking — The Underrated Multiplier
As a senior dev, I can tell you: at least 50% of hires come through referrals at most companies. A referral skips the ATS black hole entirely.

LinkedIn: Connect with engineers at companies you want. Message genuinely: "I saw your post about X — I'm working on something similar. Would love your perspective."
GitHub: Contribute to open source projects used by companies you're targeting
Tech meetups & Discord communities: Be helpful, answer questions, be visible
Ask for referrals directly — most engineers are happy to refer strong candidates for a bonus

The Mindset That Separates Hired from Not Hired

"Senior engineers aren't looking for perfection. They're looking for someone who thinks clearly, communicates their reasoning, and doesn't panic when stuck."

When you're stuck in an interview — narrate your thinking out loud. Say "I'm thinking about a hashmap approach here because lookup would be O(1)..." That alone puts you in the top 30% of candidates.

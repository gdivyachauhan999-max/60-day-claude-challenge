## Day 21 - Digital Privacy Dashboard

## Objective

Generated and analyzed a Digital Privacy Dashboard using Claude AI to understand digital footprint, privacy exposure,
company data collection practices, and potential privacy risks.

## Digital Footprint Summary

## Core Scores

- Digital Footprint Score: 74/100 (Significant Exposure)
- Privacy Score: 38/100 (Fair – Needs Improvement)
- Total Services Used: 15
- Parent Companies Identified: 7
- Ecosystem Concentration Score: 68/100
- Estimated Tracking Surface: ~11

## Exposure Heatmap Findings

## Critical Exposure Areas

- Location Data
- Behavioral Data
- Interest Profiling

## High Exposure Areas

- Identity Information
- Contacts
- Financial Information
- Content Activity
- Voice / Audio Data

## Medium Exposure Areas

- Device Information
- Health Related Signals

## Company Exposure Ranking

1. Google (Alphabet)
2. Meta Platforms
3. ByteDance
4. Snap Inc.
5. Amazon
6. Discord Inc.
7. Spotify AB

## Risk Radar Analysis

## Highest risk categories:

- Social Media
- Search Intent
- Photo / Biometric Data
- Commerce Activity
- Communication Data

## Medium risk categories:

- Gaming
- Finance
- Media Consumption

## Digital Twin Profile (Estimates)

## Facts

- 15 services declared
- Multiple social, entertainment, shopping, communication, and payment applications used

## Estimates

- Age Group: 16–28 years
- Region: India (Urban Area)
- Entertainment Profile: High Media Consumer
- Shopping Behaviour: Value Conscious Online User
- Social Connectivity: High Multi-Platform Usage
- Gaming Activity: Moderate to High
- Payment Behaviour: Digital-First User

## Most Valuable Data Assets

- Search Intent History
- Facial & Biometric Data
- Financial Transaction Patterns
- Social Graph & Contact Network
- Entertainment Preferences
- Purchase History

## Privacy Improvement Plan

1. Revoke unnecessary location permissions.
2. Move sensitive conversations to encrypted platforms.
3. Disable facial recognition where possible.
4. Review TikTok and social media permissions.
5. Enable App Privacy Reports.
6. Use privacy-focused browsers.
7. Review Google account privacy settings.
8. Periodically delete unused app data.

## Key Learnings

- A digital footprint can reveal significant personal information.
- Multiple platforms contribute to cross-platform tracking.
- Search, payment, photo, and social activity create highly valuable datasets.
- Privacy scores can be improved through permission management and privacy-focused tools.
- Ecosystem concentration increases data exposure risks.

## Conclusion

The dashboard demonstrated how digital services collectively create a large digital footprint.
While the reported privacy level is fair, reducing unnecessary permissions and limiting cross-platform data sharing can significantly improve privacy and security.
